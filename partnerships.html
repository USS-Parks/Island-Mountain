Partnerships

We Don't Have a Catalog to Defend

Island Mountain sells no hardware line and no model. That makes us an easy partner for the people who do, and a useful one for anybody who lands work we're better equipped to deliver.

Why This Works

Stack-agnostic means we're not competing with you

Most deployment shops are attached to a product. They lead with what they carry, and every conversation quietly bends toward that inventory. We don't have inventory. We run an on-site immersion and Discovery, find where AI genuinely helps, then install whatever serves it: two DGX Sparks and a few laptops, professional cards in a chassis, or H100 and H200 capacity with adjacent data racks.

That's an awkward business model in some ways. It's a very good basis for a partnership. If you sell hardware, we have no reason to steer around it. If you sell services, we're the part of the job you probably don't want to staff.

Who we work with

Channel & Integrators

Integrators, MSPs, and consultancies with regulated clients

You have the relationship and the account. What you may not have is somebody who will sit with the client's senior staff for a week, work out where AI actually fits their operation, and then stand up an air-gapped system that survives an audit.

  • We co-deliver or hand off, whichever the account calls for
  • We'll work under your paper and your brand if that's the arrangement
  • Compliance-heavy sectors are the norm here, not the exception
  • Your client keeps their relationship with you

Hardware & Infrastructure

Vendors, distributors, server builders, colo and power

Gear gets sold into regulated buildings and then sits half-configured because nobody scoped the work around the people using it. That's the failure mode we exist to prevent, and it costs you the renewal as much as it costs the client.

  • We install what the client chose, including hardware we didn't sell
  • No competing line, so no channel conflict to manage
  • We'll tell a client honestly when smaller gear is the right call
  • Deployments that actually get used are the ones that expand
How It Works

No tiers, no portal, no badge

We just retired our own product tiers, so building a partner program with tiers would be a poor joke. There's no gold level, no annual commitment, and no logo you have to earn.

What there is: a conversation about the accounts you're working, an honest read on whether we help or get in the way, and terms written for that specific arrangement. Some partnerships are one deployment. Some are a standing referral both directions. Both are fine.

We're a small shop and we run every deployment ourselves, which caps how many run at once. That's the real constraint on any partnership here, and we'd rather say it up front than discover it together in month three.
