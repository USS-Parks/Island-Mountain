On-Premise vs. Colocation vs. Cloud AI: A Decision Framework for Regulated Industries

Basho Parks

The worst AI infrastructure decision a regulated shop can make is picking the wrong framework for the decision itself, not the wrong vendor. Buy it like office equipment and it'll fail once the system touches clinical trial data under 21 CFR Part 11, HIPAA records or privileged files; after that it's a compliance posture you'll defend in every audit for the life of the system. Classify the data first and the architecture follows.

In 2024 and 2025 FDA frequently cited missing audit trails, corrupted electronic records and lack of system validation, and once AI use is widespread regulators will scrutinize how companies control AI data flows (IntuitionLabs). That last part's a prediction, and I'd bet the house on it.

On-premises means your hardware lives in your facility and the data never leaves it. The deciding factor for a Part 11 lab's the audit trail. FDA inspectors keep writing observations for unvalidated spreadsheets, uncontrolled user access and missing audit trails (IntuitionLabs); on your own hardware you write the logging, access controls and validation documentation yourself, and nothing rests on somebody else's SOC 2 report.

Colocation's the middle option, and regulated shops underuse it. You own the hardware; a third-party data center sells you power, cooling, physical security and a network, and that's all. Your data stays in systems you physically control (WhiteFiber). It's the right call when compliance demands physical sovereignty and your own building can't carry the load.

Cloud passes the compliance test when three conditions hold at once: the data's de-identified or synthetic; the provider offers a scoped Business Associate Agreement and a FedRAMP-authorized environment where it applies; and your validated system documentation accounts for the inherited controls. All three; two isn't a passing grade. It collapses the instant identified clinical data enters the workflow, and most labs can't guarantee it never will.

So match the architecture to each workload's risk class (TechTarget). Identified subject data and IRB-governed datasets belong on infrastructure under your direct control, on-premises or colocation, period. De-identified research data, internal analytics and test systems with no production PHI can go to the cloud. Where classification's uncertain, write down why you chose what you chose and what covers the residual risk, before somebody asks.

Classify your workloads before you call any vendor, us included; for help with the map, reach us.
