Tribal Data Sovereignty and Cloud AI: You Cannot Possess What Somebody Else Is Holding

Basho Parks

OCAP's got four principles and the fourth one settles the argument. Ownership, Control, and Access are claims a tribe can assert on paper. Possession is a physical fact about where the data sits and who can walk over and touch it. I've never seen a cloud agreement that changed a physical fact. That's the whole problem with cloud AI for tribal data; reading the terms of service harder doesn't solve it.

The First Nations Information Governance Centre published OCAP. The CARE Principles (Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, Ethics) sit alongside it and govern how the data gets used. People treat them as one framework. They aren't the same kind of document: a vendor can be held to CARE. Possession's an infrastructure requirement; contracts govern what a party's permitted to do, never what it's physically able to do. The IHS Tribal Data Governance Guidelines from 2021 recommend local infrastructure for systems holding Protected Health Information, the one recommendation that makes Possession operable.

An EOC in an active event generates incident reports faster than one emergency manager with a radio in the other hand can read them; that's a real AI problem and a live record of what happened on tribal land during a declared emergency. The THPO holds site data that's gotten ancestors dug up when it traveled. A clinic pushing clinical notes through a cloud model feeds PHI into whatever HIPAA posture the vendor's paperwork claims. Language preservation carries a long history of Indigenous knowledge collected and monetized by people who contributed nothing to it.

Cloud AI's cheaper at the front: API fees for a clinic-sized workload run $500 to $1,000 a month, they never stop, and they climb, so a tribe that saves a little and gives up possession has made a bad trade at a discount. Bring it to council as a sovereignty question; councils handle those better than procurement. Find the department that owns the pain, usually health, emergency management, or fiscal, and get us in the building.

Put the infrastructure on tribal land, run by tribal staff, and OCAP stops being a document a council adopted and starts being a description of where the servers are.
