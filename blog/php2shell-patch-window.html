PHP2Shell and the Twelve-Hour Patch Window

Basho Parks

PHP2Shell's patch window was negative. Amazon caught someone probing for it four days before PHP shipped a fix, and working exploits hit servers about twelve hours ahead of the release. If your website, intake form or client portal runs on PHP, you were exposed before anyone could tell you to patch.

What broke

It's two bugs in PHP core, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, both rated 9.2 under CVSS v4. Researchers showed unauthenticated remote code execution on Apache with mod_php at default settings, and Wiz says they're in every PHP release of the last decade.

Machines found it first

Calif, OpenAI and Wiz found both bugs with AI models, and Chainguard says it found the same pair in one night with a frontier model. The embargo didn't survive a few days of rumor. Cloudflare counted hundreds of thousands of attack attempts within two hours of disclosure, and I'd wager the next one moves faster.

What to do this week

You'll want 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27 or 8.5.12, and I'd patch the internet-facing boxes first. Until then, set session.upload_progress.enabled to 0 in php.ini or the FPM pool config and restart the workers; that's the path most attacks use. Then assume you've been hit. Pull logs back to October 17 and rotate every secret a PHP worker could read.

The AI next door

If your AI assistant shares a server with your intake form, its provider key's one exploit away from someone else's account. Running AI on your own hardware doesn't exempt you; it makes the patch yours. We keep the AI tier off the public web stack, give every agent a credential that expires with its task, and rehearse the update path with your team before they need it.

Summary: PHP2Shell's bugs were found with AI and exploited before the patch shipped; patch now, turn off session upload progress until you do, and rotate every secret PHP could've read.
