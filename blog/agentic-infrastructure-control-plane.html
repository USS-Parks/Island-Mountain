Agentic Infrastructure Is Shedding Its Scaffolding. The Controls Have to Land Somewhere.

Basho Parks

The controls that decide what an agent can touch are falling out of application code into the infrastructure under it. Yesterday I watched Anthropic's platform team gift every regulated shop its next security blueprint. Watch where the roadmap goes: agents minting their own service accounts, agents calling other agents over MCP, the scaffolding around the model getting deleted, ambient agents that wake up on a trigger and walk up holding a finished pull request, and work you hand off like takeout with a budget stapled to it. I see all of those as a convenience, and every one of them picks a security control up off the application-code floor and reburies it in the infrastructure underneath.

Better models retired the flowchart that kept a dim model on the rails, and nobody'll miss it. But that flowchart was often the only thing deciding what the system could touch, and prompt logic is a polite suggestion the instant a model gets sweet-talked out of it. What if your name's on the risk? A system prompt is a request and a sandbox is a fact. The controls that hold are deterministic and live outside the model, the whole argument of my agentic orchestration and security map, and when the scaffolding thins they fall down the stack onto five things.

Identity->Metering->Egress->Session integrity->Audit.

On a managed platform that whole enforcement plane sits inside the vendor's building, and for most of the market that's just fine. Now run it in a building the data can't leave. Agent memory fills with whatever it touches: privileged case strategy at a law firm, PHI at a medical practice, CUI at a defense contractor. And the audit ledger sits under the vendor's retention policy and somebody else's subpoena.

Same architecture, your building: open-weight models on hardware you own do the inference, memory and agent-to-agent traffic stay on your network, and every Island Mountain deployment ships with the Agentic Orchestration Governance layer and the Woven Security and Governance Fabric already in the crate. On an air-gapped box an ambient agent that can't reach the internet can't leak to it.

For organizations whose data isn't allowed to leave, the question's never been whether those controls exist - it's whose building they're in.
