OpenAI Discovery Risk: Why Law Firms Face Subpoena Exposure with Cloud AI

Basho Parks

Updated October 28, 2026: The May 13, 2025 preservation order lifted for most consumers in late 2025; on January 5, 2026, Judge Stein affirmed production of a 20-million-log de-identified sample.

Two SDNY courts have made it plain: what your attorneys type into a cloud AI service is discoverable, sits on a stranger's docket and isn't privileged. A vendor's retention is governed by the vendor's docket, not your firm's; your litigation holds and ethics walls don't reach a server you don't own.

On May 13, 2025, Judge Ona Wang ordered OpenAI to preserve every output log it'd otherwise delete (Hodder Law). OpenAI normally purges deleted chats within 30 days; under the order, manually deleted consumer chats sat on its servers for months (Harris Beach Murtha). The news plaintiffs then won a 20-million-log sample (NatLawReview); Judge Sidney Stein affirmed it on January 5, 2026, and it's in production now. The courts called "de-identified" adequate protection; I wouldn't bet a client relationship on it.

United States v. Heppner

On February 10, 2026, Judge Jed Rakoff ruled Bradley Heppner's exchanges with Claude neither privileged nor work product (Harvard Law Review). Already subpoenaed and represented, he'd drafted his defense strategy with Claude and handed it to his lawyers (Chapman and Cutler LLP). Rakoff agreed with the government that sharing privileged communications with a third-party AI may waive privilege over the underlying communications (Jones Walker LLP). A defense strategy prepared for counsel became a prosecution exhibit; that's the structural problem I keep writing about.

Lawyers' AI use nearly tripled in a year, 11% to 30%, per the ABA's Legal Technology Survey. A paralegal runs a confidential memo through online AI for a spell-check (LeanLaw); that isn't misconduct and it's on nobody's risk register. Under the hold, that memo sat on OpenAI's servers for months; nobody at the firm knew, least of all the paralegal, who'd been careful. Both OpenAI's and Anthropic's policies permit disclosure under legal process; opting out of training doesn't touch that (Jones Walker LLP). The preservation obligation attaches to whoever's holding the data, so the fix sits where prompts never leave the building and the logs are yours alone.

If you can't say what your attorneys typed into ChatGPT last week, or where it lives now, reach us about moving inference in-house.
