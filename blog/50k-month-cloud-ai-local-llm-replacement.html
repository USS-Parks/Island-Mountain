$50K a Month on Cloud AI Is a Hardware Problem, Not a Budget Problem

Basho Parks

Updated October 28, 2026: The May 2026 prices and model names have since moved; the Superlinked post carries a later figure; DeepSeek V4-Flash's active parameters are corrected to 13 billion.

Local hardware replaces a $50,000-a-month cloud AI invoice, and it isn't close. As of May 2026, Claude Sonnet 4.6 runs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output (Anthropic Pricing), and $50,000 a month at those rates is rent, all of it: $600,000 a year, $3,664,000 over five at 10 percent annual escalation. Every token that leaves your network takes your code and client files with it, and under HIPAA, attorney-client privilege, ITAR or tribal data sovereignty that's the whole argument.

The forum thread asking whether two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwells run GLM-5.1 in BF16 priced the problem by the card, and that's the mistake. The card's a serious part at 96GB, but GLM-5.1 is a 754-billion parameter mixture-of-experts model, and in BF16 it wants 1.5 terabytes of VRAM just to load. Two cards give you 192GB; you're off by about 8x.

Size it to the work instead. A hundred daily users doesn't mean a hundred people on the box at once; peak concurrency for that headcount runs 15 to 30 sessions. DeepSeek V4-Flash, a 284-billion parameter MoE with 13 billion active per token, fits inside 192GB quantized and lands in Sonnet's tier for most enterprise work. I'm not recommending it; I haven't watched your people work yet.

Ollama and vLLM aren't a matter of taste at 50 concurrent users. Ollama allocates GPU memory statically and skips continuous batching, so at 50 users time-to-first-response climbs to roughly 3,200 milliseconds (SitePoint Benchmarks 2026). vLLM runs continuous batching and PagedAttention: same 50 users, 145 milliseconds, and 8,033 tokens per second on a 70B model in FP8 against Ollama's 484. Both belong on the machine, Ollama for model management and vLLM as the production engine; we'll deploy that stack with you, on your network.

I don't publish the ownership side because there's no standing build to price; what gets deployed falls out of Discovery, after I've sat next to the people who run the work. After the purchase your costs are electricity and maintenance, with no token fees, rate limits or overage invoice.

Run the five-year number on your own invoice before you price a card.
