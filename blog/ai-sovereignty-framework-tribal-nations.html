AI Sovereignty Framework for Tribal Nations: Why the Infrastructure Belongs Under Tribal Control

Basho Parks

Download: AI Sovereignty Framework for Tribal Nations (.docx)

Tribal data on a commercial cloud sits in corporate custody, and the CLOUD Act (18 U.S.C. § 2713) lets federal law enforcement compel the vendor to produce it; the warrant names the vendor and nobody's obliged to tell the tribe. The hook's corporate control, not geography, so the tribe gets no notice, standing to contest or government-to-government consultation. I contend there's one architecture that fixes it: AI on infrastructure the tribe owns, inside tribal jurisdiction.

OCAP, Ownership, Control, Access and Possession, has been the Indigenous information governance standard since 1998 (FNIGC, 2014). Three of those are claims on paper; Possession's a fact about where a thing sits, and the other three stand on it. Cloud AI can't satisfy Possession: process tribal data on a vendor's server and the vendor possesses it.

NCAI's Resolution NC-24-008 defines digital sovereignty as sovereign authority over physical and virtual network infrastructure and over the acquisition, storage and use of data (NCAI, 2024). The operative words are physical and virtual. Run tribal records through a commercial cloud and that authority's handed to a vendor; by NCAI's own definition the tribe isn't exercising digital sovereignty.

Emergency management is the case I'd take to council. The Stafford Act (42 U.S.C. §§ 5121-5207) lets tribal nations request disaster declarations directly; run the Preliminary Damage Assessment through cloud AI mid-incident and your counterpart can read the document your claim rests on before anybody's sat down. A tribal clinic's a HIPAA Covered Entity, and a vendor's Business Associate Agreement under 45 C.F.R. § 164.504(e) binds the vendor to the tribe; the CLOUD Act binds the vendor to the federal government, and when those conflict the contract doesn't get a vote.

The Yurok Tribe's YTEL broke ground in July 2025 on 62 miles of fiber it owns and runs (Yurok Tribe, July 2025), and on-premise AI's the compute layer that belongs on it. If you're on council, authorize on-premise AI as a digital sovereignty assertion and say so in the motion; inventory every data category moving through cloud AI, then migrate emergency management, health and governance data first.

Doctrine Statement: Tribal AI processing of sensitive data, health records, incident data and governance deliberations among them, runs on tribally owned, on-premise infrastructure within tribal jurisdiction, with no third-party corporate custody, whatever a vendor represents about compliance.
