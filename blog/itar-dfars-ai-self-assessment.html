ITAR and DFARS AI Self-Assessment: Can Your AI Infrastructure Pass an Audit?

Basho Parks

Updated October 28, 2026: CMMC Phase 2 was suspended on July 13, 2026 with no replacement date; the Phase 1 self-assessment, NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 and DFARS 252.204-7012 still bind.

Your team's already using AI, and some of it's touching CUI from a browser tab you don't control.

Somebody pasted contract language into ChatGPT this week, and the CUI in it sat outside your CMMC boundary inside five seconds. (Openapproach) That's a spillage you can't undo (VSO), and most contractors don't find out until a C3PAO assessor asks about data handling.

ITAR runs civil penalties to $1,271,078 per violation or twice the transaction's value, and criminal to $1 million and twenty years. (PreVeil) ITAR also governs who's allowed to touch controlled data, and a commercial AI platform employs people outside the United States with access to the infrastructure processing your prompt. (Iternal) That's a deemed export.

In 2024 Raytheon paid DOJ and SEC more than $950 million over AECA and ITAR violations (Venable), then $8.4 million more in May 2025 under the False Claims Act for certifying cybersecurity controls it hadn't implemented. (ConsensusDocs) In December 2025 a machine shop paid about $421,000 over unprotected technical drawings, in a qui tam its former quality manager filed. (Holland & Knight)

The self-assessment starts with inventory: every AI tool, commercial assistants on work devices included, and whether each touches CUI; if it does and the backend isn't FedRAMP-authorized, the tool goes. (Washington Technology) Your SSP then carries each tool as an in-scope asset, your acceptable use policy names the authorized ones, and you train your people, because the engineer pasting a drawing at midnight isn't reading either.

An architecture you control physically, inside your boundary, clears DFARS and CMMC for CUI; it ends the cloud transmission, the deemed export and the FedRAMP chase, and it leaves an audit trail you can hand an assessor. Hardware isn't the certification, though: sign the annual CMMC affirmation without verifying it and you can be accused of reckless disregard sufficient for False Claims Act liability. (Holland & Knight) Owning the box makes the AI portion of that affirmation provable instead of argued.

If you can't show an assessor that CUI never touched an unauthorized system, that's the contract gone; reach us before they ask.
