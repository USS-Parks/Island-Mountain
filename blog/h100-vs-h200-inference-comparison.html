RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell vs. H100 Hopper: Why On-Premises AI Moved to Blackwell

Basho Parks

Updated October 28, 2026: This April 2026 post made a one-card case; what's built now comes out of Discovery, and the sizing method lives in the concurrency and right-sizing posts.

The H100 is the faster card, and it still loses on cost per token. I'll concede its edge first: 2,000 GB/s of memory bandwidth against 1,597 on the RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition, about 25 percent more. The Blackwell part answers with 96GB against 80GB and a new card's warranty, at April 2026 street prices of roughly $8,000 to $9,200 against $25,000 to $33,000 for a refurbished H100 PCIe (NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 specifications).

Why Bandwidth Sets the Pace

Generating one token at a time (the decode phase) means reading every active weight out of VRAM, and moving those bytes across the memory bus takes far longer than the multiply. That's your single-stream ceiling: tokens per second ≈ memory bandwidth ÷ active weight bytes, times 50 to 70 percent for kernel overhead, attention and KV-cache traffic. It'll work on any card with a published bandwidth figure.

DeepSeek V4-Flash activates about 13 billion of its 284 billion parameters per token, roughly 13GB at FP8, so 1,597 / 13 ≈ 123 tokens per second in theory and a realistic 60 to 90 single-stream (estimate). A dense 70B at FP8 moves the full ~70GB for every token, about 23 per card. Batching raises aggregate throughput, but per-user speed's still bandwidth-bound.

Where the 25 Percent Does and Doesn't Matter

By that roofline the H100 buys roughly 25 percent more single-stream speed. When a workload already clears 60 to 90 tokens per second, that shortens a wait that wasn't long. You'd pay three to four times the price per card to recover 25 percent, and hand back 16GB of VRAM doing it. A concurrency-heavy shop can still close that math; the arithmetic above will say so.

What Fits Where

VRAM's the gate. V4-Flash at FP8 wants roughly 170GB with all 284B parameters resident and room for the KV cache; at FP16 it wants 384GB. A dense 70B at FP8 is about 70GB, so it'll sit on a single 96GB card. Divide by what your card carries and the memory half's sized.

I haven't been in your building, so which card your shop lands on is a Discovery finding, and the arithmetic's yours whether you reach us or not.
