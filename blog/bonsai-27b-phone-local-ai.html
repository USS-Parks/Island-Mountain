Bonsai 27B Runs on a Phone. The Sovereignty Math Just Moved Again.

Basho Parks

Somebody Put a 27B Model in a Pocket

Look, I called this direction back in June - micro-scale inference, the sovereign edge - and had the timetable off by about a year. On July 14 PrismML shipped Bonsai 27B and led with "the first 27B-class model to run on a phone." Weights went up on Hugging Face the same day, Apache 2.0.

They took Qwen3.6 27B, a multimodal model averaging 85.0 across their fifteen-benchmark suite, and rounded every weight down to almost nothing. The ternary build keeps three values per weight, 1.71 effective bits with group scaling, and lands at 5.9 GB holding 95% of baseline. The 1-bit build throws out the zero: 1.125 bits, 3.9 GB, 90% held. And it's the whole model that got squeezed, vision tower included.

It's quick, too: 163 tokens a second on an RTX 5090 for the 1-bit, 87 on an M5 Max. And the phone claim's literal: roughly four gigabytes resident on an iPhone 17 Pro.

The Radio-Off Test Fits in a Pocket Now

Back in May I called airplane mode the compliance test. If the model answers with the radio off, your data hasn't left. As of mid-July that test runs on a handset, and the handset can see.

I'd slow down on the seeing, because reading a photo on the device means it never reaches anybody's API. The adjuster standing in six inches of water, shooting the water line and the subfloor, drafts the file note right there and the claim file doesn't enter a third party's building. What goes where in your shop still comes out of Discovery, sitting next to whoever's run the workflow for twenty years.

Read the Category Table Before You Get Excited

That 90% headline's an average, and averages hide things. Math holds 96% of baseline even at 1-bit, because answers you can check survive rounding. The agentic column pays for the compression: tool-calling goes 80.0 to 74.0 ternary, then 66.0 at 1-bit; instruction following, 78.4 to 65.8; vision gives up thirteen points. So for a model driving tools through a long loop unsupervised, the 1-bit build isn't ready; bench the ternary. Every retention number here's a vendor claim until somebody independent reruns it.

Couple things the announcement leaves out. An agent's needs don't shrink on the way from the rack to the pocket: identity, egress, metering, audit, a kill switch, and the controls that hold still live outside the model. And 262K context's a spec-sheet number; long context eats working memory the compression never bought back.

American Compression, Chinese Base Model

I've spent real ink asking for an independently funded American open-weight lab. Bonsai's American engineering released Apache 2.0 with weights first. Good on PrismML, genuinely and seriously. It's also built on Qwen3.6, and procurement's provenance conversation doesn't end because the compression happened in Pasadena. The compression work's here; the funded base-model work still isn't.

The Bench Gets Deeper

None of this changes the pairing I keep landing on with regulated shops: one big organizational model for the hard reasoning, a bench of small local specialists for the repetitive work, all owned outright. Bonsai stretches that bench to the field and gives it eyes.

So pull the ternary build down this week and run it against one workflow you already understand cold. Then ask the question that hasn't changed in three years of this: whose building the controls sit in. Reach us when you get to that one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Bonsai 27B?

PrismML's extreme-quantized Qwen3.6 27B under Apache 2.0: ternary at 5.9 GB holding 95 percent of baseline, 1-bit at 3.9 GB holding 90.

Can a 27B model really run on a phone?

Yes. The 1-bit build's roughly four gigabytes resident on an iPhone 17 Pro.

How much accuracy does 1-bit quantization cost?

About ten percent of the average, unevenly: math keeps 96 percent while tool-calling drops 80.0 to 66.0 and vision gives up thirteen points.

Should I run the ternary build or the 1-bit build?

Ternary if the work touches tools, 74.0 against 66.0 on tool-calling; 1-bit when footprint's the binding constraint and a human reads every output.

Is a quantized model on a phone enough for HIPAA or ITAR work?

Model size has nothing to do with it. Hardware you control, data that never leaves it, and the controls outside the model decide that; hardware's a component of a compliant program, never a certification.

Summary: PrismML's Bonsai 27B compresses a 27B multimodal model to 3.9 GB at 1.125 bits per weight, keeping 90 percent of its benchmark average under Apache 2.0. Agentic categories take the deepest cuts, so test the ternary build and keep the governance outside the model.
