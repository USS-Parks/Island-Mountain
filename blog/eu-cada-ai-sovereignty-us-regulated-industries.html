Europe Just Defined AI Sovereignty. The U.S. Is Still Pretending It Doesn't Apply to Us.

Basho Parks

Updated October 28, 2026: CADA is still a proposal; the Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, moved high-risk duties to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028.

Europe just wrote down, in four numbered tiers, what American compliance teams have spent years refusing to say out loud.

On June 3, 2026, the European Commission proposed the Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA), COM(2026) 502: a four-tier sovereignty framework for public-sector cloud and AI whose top tier shuts out any provider subject to foreign law. Tier 4, the defense and law-enforcement tier, wants hardware, software, people and jurisdiction all EU-originating. U.S. providers can't reach it because of the CLOUD Act (18 U.S.C. § 2713), which lets Washington compel American companies to produce data wherever it's stored.

CADA won't bind you. It names what American regulated industries have tolerated for years: a tribal government's records on AWS or a defense contractor's CUI in a cloud LLM sit where their own federal government can reach them through a provider-level order, without notice. Europe drew Tier 4 around that. American regulators haven't, and the exposure's identical.

A tribal government on a U.S.-headquartered cloud has surrendered Possession, the P in OCAP, by operation of law and without a council vote. ABA Model Rule 1.6 asks for "reasonable" efforts, and now that local AI's on the market, privileged material in a third-party cloud is a harder position to call reasonable than two years ago.

A compelled disclosure that touches a foreign national is a deemed export under ITAR, and CMMC isolation is only fully met off the cloud. 45 C.F.R. § 164.312 wants technical measures around ePHI, and a Business Associate Agreement moves liability; it doesn't move the data.

Any major cloud AI service, OpenAI's API or AWS Bedrock, puts you at Tier 1 at best: domestic infrastructure and a provider that's subject to U.S. law. Tier 1 doesn't hold for PHI, CUI, client confidences or tribal proprietary data.

We deploy air-gapped inference on site with the Woven Security & Governance Fabric built in, and the CLOUD Act can't compel data a cloud provider doesn't have. What you don't get is a certification; local hardware kills one category of risk, third-party custody, and leaves every other obligation where it was.

Europe named it. I'm watching whether the American organizations carrying the same exposure have noticed.
