Sovereign AI, Explained

The basics, in plain terms.

What is local AI, and how is it different from cloud AI?

It's a server on your premises that runs AI models, so prompts and answers never leave your network. You'll own the hardware and the open-weight models, and the tradeoff's that open releases trail proprietary ones by weeks to months.

Does sovereign AI hardware work fully air-gapped, with no internet?

Yes: the models, serving software and interface run on your network with every outbound feature off, and your IT team can confirm it. New models and security updates cross the gap as verified bundles your team controls, and the updates aren't optional.

What does a forward deployment look like, and how long are you on-site?

We sit with the people who do the job until we've seen the exceptions, and that study becomes the system design before anyone names hardware. One department's a short stay, a dozen handoffs take longer, and we'll train your team before we leave.

Who should NOT buy sovereign AI hardware?

If you need the newest proprietary model on release day, or your data isn't sensitive and cloud AI's fine for a few casual users, it won't earn its keep. It belongs where cloud creates exposure: regulated, privileged or sovereign data and governed agentic workflows.

The Security Fabric

Woven security & governance.

What is the Woven Security & Governance Fabric, and where does it run?

It's the trust and control plane, built as Lamprey, that puts identity, guardrails, approval gates and an audit ledger on every tool call an agent makes. It'll run cloud-side or air-gapped, and the record of who approved each sensitive action stays in your custody.

How does the fabric stop prompt injection and other agentic attacks?

The controls live outside the model, so a tricked agent still has to get each action past identity checks and approval gates, and the ledger records it. Under zero standing privilege, credentials are minted per operation and revoked after, so a compromise can't reach far.

Compliance & Sovereignty

The regulated-data questions.

We're an engineering practice, not a compliance authority, and nothing here is legal advice.

Does running AI locally comply with HIPAA?

PHI never leaves your premises for a third party's servers, and the fabric supplies the audit trail. Access controls, encryption at rest and staff training are still yours, and we don't certify HIPAA compliance.

Can a law firm use local AI without waiving attorney-client privilege?

Client data sent to a third-party API is disclosed to a third party, and ABA Model Rule 1.6 makes preventing that your duty. Local AI keeps every prompt and document on a server in your office, leaving only your own policy on who uses it.

Can a tribal government run AI without cloud providers?

Yes: it runs on tribal premises, on hardware and weights the nation owns outright, under no cloud terms of service and no outside jurisdiction. It'll run air-gapped if you want, and we read each model's license with tribal counsel before production.

Does local AI meet ITAR and CMMC requirements?

Hardware you own, on US soil with US-person-only access, keeps controlled technical data away from foreign access, and the open-weight models aren't ITAR-controlled. We don't provide ITAR or CMMC certification, but the fabric supplies the audit evidence behind it.

The On-Premise Hardware

Hardware, models, and how it scales.

What hardware do we end up with, and can you use gear we own?

Whatever Discovery says the work needs, from a couple of desktop-class machines to a rack-mounted estate, and nobody can size that from a phone call. Gear you already own, like DGX Spark or Strix Halo boxes, gets wired in if it fits, and we'll tell you if it doesn't.

Which models do we get, and are we stuck with them?

Discovery picks open-weight models you own outright, run on your GPUs with nothing sent to the lab, and swap as better ones ship. Licenses vary: some are MIT or Apache 2.0, and Meta's Llama-family licenses carry use restrictions, so we'll read each one with your counsel before production.

What does it need from our facility?

Power, cooling, network and floor space, set by the configuration: a desktop-class machine wants a wall outlet, while rack-mounted accelerators want a dedicated circuit, server-room airflow and a rack. We'll scope it with your facilities people before anything's ordered.

Quotes, Delivery & Trust

Owning it, human to human.

How does pricing work, and how long until we're running?

Pricing comes after Discovery: a scoping call and time on-site produce a written quote for a one-time hardware purchase with no subscription, per-token or licensing fees. Lead time's roughly 3 to 5 weeks, overlapping the workflow study, and you'll get 30 days of support from the engineer who built it.

You're a new company. What if a part fails, or you go out of business?

Every system ships with a 1-year hardware warranty, and we diagnose failures remotely and replace parts through supplier RMA agreements. If we closed you'd lose that support, but the open-source software needs no license or connection to us, and any competent Linux administrator can maintain it.

Why pay you when I could build a similar server myself?

If you're a developer running experiments, you probably should, since the software's the same. A regulated organization in production pays for what a DIY box lacks: procurement records an auditor can follow, an RMA chain that keeps replacements in that trail, and direct access to the builder.
